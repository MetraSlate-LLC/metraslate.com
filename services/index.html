Consultation for Program Health

A straight read on where your program stands: what is covered, what is overdue, and what to fix first.

Read More

We check the things an assessor actually finds, not a generic checklist:

  • Intervals set by history and risk, not a blanket 12 months applied to everything because nobody ever revisited it.
  • Reference standards with current certificates. One expired reference can invalidate every calibration performed against it since its own due date, so it is checked first, not last.
  • Uncertainty on the certificate, not just a measured value. A result without a stated uncertainty does not satisfy Z540.3, and it is the single most common gap we find.
  • Traceability that actually chains: in programs that scaled quickly, the paperwork often lags the equipment it is supposed to cover.

For a program inherited without full documentation, or going into an accreditation renewal with more questions than answers: a written report scoped to your service levels and your accreditation, in language an auditor can read as easily as you can.

Service Levels and the Certificate

The service level sets the rigor, and you pay for exactly what the job needs.

Read More

Service levels are set to the job, not picked off a fixed menu. Four common examples:

NIST Traceable

Traceable calibration against a named standard.

Traceable With Data

Plus the measured data itself, as found and as left.

Traceable With Data and Uncertainty

Plus the measurement uncertainty, worked and stated.

Traceable With Data, Uncertainty, and Guardbanding

Plus a guardbanded pass or fail decision.

The 4:1 test uncertainty ratio is the old rule of thumb, and it still works when the equipment supports it. When it does not, which is common on tight-tolerance instruments, guardbanding is the modern answer: it tightens the acceptance limit by a share of the measurement uncertainty instead of pretending the uncertainty is not there, and it is how ISO/IEC 17025's decision-rule requirement gets satisfied in practice. We tell you which situation you are in before we quote the level, not after.

Every certificate names who performed the calibration, on what instrument, against which standard, and when. Turnaround is quoted per job.
