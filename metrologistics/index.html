For equipment owners

Four Reasons to Start Here.

Calibration and testing

The sourcing process

From Equipment List to an Agreed Job.

A familiar lab, a new supplier or several bids. The buyer keeps the choice.

  1. 01

    Define the Work

    Select or prepare an equipment list. Include the model, measurement functions, range, required accuracy, service location and target date.

  2. 02

    Choose Suitable Laboratories

    Compare relevant capabilities. Confirm the specific scope with the laboratory; a category match alone does not establish that every required measurement is covered.

  3. 03

    Compare the Whole Quote

    Review price alongside turnaround, exclusions, shipping, on-site arrangements and the documents you will receive. Resolve questions before approving the work.

  4. 04

    Keep the Agreement with the Equipment

    Carry the accepted scope, provider and service reference into the equipment history so the next service cycle starts with context.

For laboratories

Requests You Can Actually Assess.

A service request should tell a lab enough to decide whether to quote.

Instrument identity, ranges, service requirements, quantity, location and timing belong in the request. The lab can identify missing information, quote the work it can perform and explain any exclusions before the customer commits.

The product direction includes responding through Standard or a focused lab workspace. A buyer can also work with an existing provider outside the network. Participation and data sharing are deliberate choices.
