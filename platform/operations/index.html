Pilot / Early Access Standard by MetraSlate: Metrology Edition. How Evaluation Works

Instrument Progress

The instrument-level line is the useful unit of service progress. The team can distinguish equipment awaiting receipt, work under way and equipment waiting for the next decision without treating the order header as a substitute for every line's state.

The work record gives customer service, laboratory staff and operations a common reference. A representative can inspect the same job the technician is working from and explain which instrument needs attention and what happens next.

Collection and Return

A customer can request a pickup at an authorized site with a preferred day, collection window and notes. That request creates the lab's actual logistics stop, so dispatch can continue from the submitted record.

Pickup and delivery stops can be combined into planned routes with a driver, origin, date and ordered sequence. The route and company-routes views help staff follow the next destination and recorded progress, while the instrument's physical custody remains distinct from its calibration status.

Laboratory and Onsite Work

Service location belongs to the order and its lines. Choosing a customer site connects the work to a place already recorded for that account, while a line can inherit the order's location when that is appropriate.

For disconnected field work, selected lines travel in a package containing their equipment and technical context. Captured observations return to Standard for reconciliation and uncertainty evaluation before becoming part of the completed laboratory record.

Review and Release

The manual-calibration event records the observations and supporting measurement evidence for the instrument. Completion preserves the evaluation basis, and technical review and certificate release continue from that recorded work.

Operations can then coordinate the equipment's return while the customer-facing team follows the related service and account context. The job remains understandable across the bench, the dispatch desk and the customer conversation.
