Standard is designed with SOC 2 compliance in mind. Organizational identity, company isolation, application permissions and protected connection credentials are part of the architecture. These controls give customers concrete mechanisms to examine during a security review and to connect with their own operating responsibilities.

WorkOS-backed identity connects the organization to supported sign-in and enterprise identity administration. Single Sign-On, directory synchronization and domain administration use the available setup and entitlements. The application resolves company membership and permissions alongside that identity, so signing in and authorizing a consequential business action remain separate decisions.

Company scope follows the request into data access. Tenant-scoped connections require an explicit company context, and database policies constrain company records. Application authorization distinguishes laboratory members, platform operators and portal customers, with additional role and privilege checks where the operation requires them.

Customer portal access follows the account and permitted sites. Internal conversations, customer-service conversations, released documents and technical approvals each have their own access rules. The person who may read a record does not automatically acquire permission to change, approve or release it.

Connection credentials are encrypted with tenant-bound authenticated encryption and stored with a key version so supported rotation can preserve the connection. Administrative summaries show identifying metadata rather than the secret itself. Company message policies govern editing, deletion and retention, while file and real-time access use their own checks.

Deployment configuration, provider choices and organizational operating procedures complete the security design. Identity, permissions, tenant boundaries and retained evidence give the organization concrete controls to examine during its own security review.

The customer relationship has its own access boundary.

A portal contact receives the records allowed for that customer and its permitted sites. Customer-service staff reach a conversation through the line's current responsibility rules, while internal team channels use their own membership rules. The application checks access at the relevant record and communication boundary.

A lab, regional or company view helps a person focus operational information. It does not replace the permissions that determine what that person may read or do.

Security review should examine the actual deployment.

The deployment determines the identity provider, connection credentials, model endpoints and external services in use. An organization with strict data-control requirements can review those flows alongside application permissions, retention and operational controls.

A local model can address one part of that design. Mailbox providers, voice and video services, identity, monitoring and backups each retain their own responsibilities and data boundaries. No model choice supplies automatic compliance.

Organizational identity
Connect supported enterprise sign-in and directory administration through WorkOS, with the organization's configured identity setup and application entitlements.
Explicit application authority
Control sensitive operations through member, administrator, operator and customer policies. Role privileges and CSRF checks protect the applicable actions.
Company data boundaries
Resolve company scope before tenant data access and apply database row policies to company records. Operational lab views have a separate purpose from authorization.
Protected connection credentials
Store integration secrets with authenticated encryption, tenant-bound additional data and key versions that support rotation. Expose connection summaries without returning plaintext credentials.
Accountable business records
Retain the actor, revision and workflow context behind consequential decisions, including the controlled technical review and release lifecycle.
