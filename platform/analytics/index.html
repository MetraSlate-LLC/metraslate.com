Analytics and operating cost

How the workflow operates.

Standard's reporting supports list and aggregate reports over registered subjects, with selected measures, groupings, filters and periods. Managers can examine contributing records where the report exposes them and export the result. The period, grouping and source matter because a dashboard total alone cannot explain a change in performance.

Timekeeping and job-linked labor provide the effort side of service cost. Stock consumption, purchasing and billing provide additional resource and revenue context. The laboratory maintains the applicable rates and allocation policy so it can compare like services, investigate rework, assess capacity and understand the cost of fulfilling a customer promise.

A service using three bench hours at $60, one review hour at $60 and $40 of material has $280 in direct costs. Adding $70 of allocated overhead gives a $350 cost against $500 revenue, leaving $150 after that allocation. The accuracy and completeness of this cost analysis depend on the activity the laboratory records, the rates it maintains and the costs it includes in its allocation policy.

What your team can do.

Direct service cost

Allocation and margin
Apply the chosen overhead allocation to examine contribution and service margin. Interpret the result in light of cost coverage and maintained rates.
Operational reports
Examine turnaround, overdue work, quote conversion, receivables, service mix and quality follow-up by relevant period and grouping.
Reports with a trail
Define subjects, measures, filters and groups. Inspect contributing records and export the result instead of relying on a disconnected chart.
