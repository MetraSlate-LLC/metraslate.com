The payment workflow follows the laboratory's policy and the customer's agreement. The customer normally provides payment information, and the laboratory processes the authorized amount when its configured readiness point is reached. Optional self-payment follows the same readiness and authorization rules.

Known service charges can be secured or collected before a selected stage, while additional work receives its own pricing and required approval. Financial readiness can release the appropriate restriction or advance the work when its remaining operational and technical conditions are satisfied.

Choose when the customer supplies a method and when the laboratory processes it.

Your laboratory sets the point at which payment information is requested and the point at which an approved obligation becomes ready for collection. These can be different stages. A customer can provide the method at quote approval while collection waits for the agreed milestone.

Staff process the authorized obligation when it is ready. Where the laboratory offers customer-initiated payment or authorized collection automation, the same approved amount and readiness policy govern the action. A saved method carries the recorded authorization; it does not make every future charge collectible.

Secure the known service amount before more exposure accumulates.

An accepted estimate provides a concrete basis for a minimum: a fixed amount, a percentage or the known price of the approved service. The laboratory chooses which stage requires that coverage and which accepted estimate revision and approved work the amount refers to.

Staff can see the requirement, what satisfies it and why work is waiting. Customers with approved credit terms can follow the account's agreed arrangement. A permitted exception records the responsible authority and reason.

Price and approve each additional commitment as it becomes concrete.

If inspection identifies a repair or further service, staff price the addition and obtain the required customer approval. The new collection requirement concerns that approved increment and its selected milestone. Possible future work remains outside the approved charge.

This connects the customer's decision, the changed service scope and the financial requirement. The laboratory can address the agreed addition before carrying out that stage while keeping the original estimate and earlier coverage in view.

Use the right form of coverage for the account.

Received funds, reserved prepaid funding, an eligible card authorization and approved credit terms have different meanings. The laboratory chooses which forms satisfy a requirement. A stored method confirms that details were provided; it does not mean funds were received or that an amount is reserved.

Account funding is allocated to the relevant work so the same available balance cannot cover two obligations at once. A provider authorization retains its amount and expiry, and the financial requirement is re-evaluated when that coverage changes. Pending, declined, expired and reversed outcomes remain visible.

Apply earlier collections once and explain the remaining balance.

Consider a $1,000 calibration with a $400 minimum before bench work. If the customer later approves a $250 repair and that amount is also received, the approved service totals $1,250 and received money totals $650. Applying those amounts once leaves $600 to settle.

The final invoice and account history show how earlier money reduces the outstanding obligation. A payment or credit can satisfy the financial condition, while a technically unfinished job remains subject to the work, review and release conditions that still need to be completed.

Configure the financial requirement around the way your laboratory works.

The laboratory chooses when to request payment information, when an approved charge becomes ready to collect and which work transition requires financial coverage. Requirements can apply at estimate acceptance, intake, bench work, certificate release, shipment or closure, as appropriate for the service and account. The laboratory's policy determines which requirements apply to the job.

The requirement identifies the accepted estimate revision, approved service lines and any separately approved additions. When the agreement changes, Standard retains the earlier obligation and decision, evaluates the revised amount and accounts for coverage already reserved or money already received. Staff and customers can see which approved amount remains due and which stage depends on it.

Keep account standing separate from the money covering this work.

Approved credit terms can permit work to proceed under the customer's account arrangement. An overdue balance or account credit hold follows the laboratory's separate standing policy. A temporary card authorization reserves a particular amount until its expiry; it has a different purpose from an account hold and from money the laboratory has received.

Staff can inspect the applicable standing rule, the job's required coverage and the reason for a restriction. An authorized override records who allowed the exception, why it applies and the affected work or amount. An exception for one account or job retains its history without silently changing the laboratory's default policy.

Resolve authentication, interruption and partial payment without losing the obligation.

A customer who needs to complete additional payment authentication can return to the same approved obligation. Pending processing, a decline or an interrupted acknowledgement remains visible to the customer and staff, with the next permitted action explained. Staff reconcile an uncertain outcome before retrying collection, so an interruption does not become a second charge.

A partial payment reduces the amount remaining by the money actually received and applied. A pending transfer or temporary authorization retains its own state. When an authorization expires or no longer covers the required amount, Standard re-evaluates the financial requirement before the protected work proceeds. Customers and staff follow the same amount, outcome and recovery history.

Carry cancellations, refunds and reversals through the account and the work.

Cancelling approved work releases the associated unused reservation or authorization through the permitted process. Where money has already been received, the refund or credit decision remains attributable and connected to the original obligation. The account distinguishes a requested refund, its processing outcome and the resulting balance.

A reversal changes the recorded coverage and can restore a financial restriction under the laboratory's policy. Standard retains the earlier receipt or application alongside the correcting entry, so staff can explain what changed. Financial recovery also respects the work's current technical, certificate and custody conditions.

Use each payment and available balance once.

Repeated payment notifications and repeated staff or customer submissions resolve to the same recorded collection outcome. A successful receipt is applied once, and a later correction retains its relationship to that receipt. Staff can reconcile the external processing outcome with the obligation and final invoice without counting a repeated notification as new money.

Prepaid funding is reserved against the work that uses it. When two jobs compete for the same available balance, allocation protects the amount already committed and identifies the requirement that still needs coverage. Changes, cancellations and reversals release or revise the appropriate allocation, keeping the account's available funds and each job's position consistent.
