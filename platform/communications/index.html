Communication is most useful when the person answering can see the work. In Standard, a representative can inspect the customer, instrument or order while discussing a pickup, a held item, a quotation or the next decision. The conversation supports the service already in progress, so another person can continue with the same context.

Team channels give a group a place to coordinate, while direct messages support a conversation between two people. Staff can reply in threads, mention a colleague, pin a useful message and share supporting files. Related-record links connect a discussion to a record the writer is permitted to name. Opening the underlying record remains subject to the reader's own access.

The customer-service line adds responsibility to the customer's conversation. Its queue shows what needs attention, who owns the response and how long the customer has waited. A representative can pick up the conversation, ask a colleague to help, hand it over and record its close. The customer's portal conversation stays connected to the account, while the laboratory controls which staff may read and answer it.

Connected mail gives staff an inbox, sent correspondence and drafts within the application. Supported provider connections allow people to read, compose, reply, reply to everyone and forward messages. Drafts and mailbox actions use the provider's own capabilities, so the same correspondence can continue through the person's normal mail tools.

Calling extends this customer context through the supported phone integration. The RingCentral integration path links observed caller information with the appropriate account and supports the representative's call controls. Call records, representative outcomes and submitted customer feedback explain what happened after the conversation.

A conversation can name the work it concerns.

A message about an instrument is more useful when the instrument has a clear identity. Related-record links let staff name relevant records in their internal discussion, while server-side checks control which records a writer may include. Customer-service conversations keep the customer's access separate from the laboratory's internal record links.

The practical benefit is a cleaner handoff. A colleague can read the discussion, identify the relevant work and continue the next action with the record in view.

Email stays connected to the mailbox.

An email draft belongs to the connected account, and supported read, archive, trash and attachment actions follow that provider. Mailbox ownership is checked before access. Staff can work from Standard while retaining the account's own correspondence and provider controls.

Sending has an identifiable outcome. A provider refusal is recorded so a representative can see that an attempted message did not succeed. A released document and successful notification remain separate facts.

In everyday use

What your team can do.

Follow the record, the decision and the person responsible for the next step.

Channels for shared work
Create a team channel, bring in the people involved and keep a discussion together through threads, mentions, pinned messages and supporting attachments.
Direct conversations
Use direct messages for two-person coordination. Membership and company scope govern the conversation, and business-record access retains its own permissions.
A customer-service queue
Keep the next response attributable to a person. Queue, ownership, invitations, handover and wrap-up help the service team act on the customer's request.
A real connected mailbox
Use supported Gmail, Microsoft 365 or IMAP/SMTP connections. Available message, draft and attachment actions follow the connected transport's capabilities.
Phone context with follow-through
Use the RingCentral integration path to match a caller to the account and retain observed call events, disposition and customer feedback with their source identified.
Company communication
Publish recorded company briefings and training for the whole company or a team channel. Staff can return to the available episodes from their working environment.
