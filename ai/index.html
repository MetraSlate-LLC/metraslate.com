The position

AI at the edges. Never in the measurement.

Most software companies are shoving AI into everything they ship. We built Standard on the opposite argument, and we think regulated industries will spend the next decade learning why.

The argument in one sentence.

A calibration record is evidence, so the measurement path is deterministic and frozen when the work completes; AI earns its place at the edges instead, where its help is real and its mistakes are catchable.

A calibration record is evidence, and evidence cannot rest on a model's guess. So the measurement path in Standard is deterministic, exact-decimal, validated against published standards, and frozen when the work completes, where an auditor can rerun it and a model can never touch it. AI earns its place at the edges instead, where its help is real and its mistakes are catchable.

Standard's workflow automation is deterministic: plain event, condition and action rules your team writes and can actually read, running the lifecycle end to end. Nothing in the system waits on a model, and it works exactly the same if you never turn one on. The enhancements are real, though: our internal communications are plumbed and ready for AI to make sure no commitment goes unscheduled, whether it is through the email inbox or the customer service portal chat. Every assist lands in a review queue first, and lands in the record attributed.
