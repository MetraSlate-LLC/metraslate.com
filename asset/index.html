Equipment: One Application of Asset

Identity, Location and History Together.

Start with the item your team recognizes. Keep the information needed to identify it, understand its recorded context and decide what needs attention.

Equipment Identity
Asset number, manufacturer, model and serial number distinguish the individual item. Check those identifiers when adding equipment or updating a record.
Location and Responsibility
Keep the recorded location and responsible organizational context with the equipment so people know where to start when arranging its next use or service.
Service and Supporting Records
Bring recorded service dates, results and supporting documents into the equipment history. Use recorded due dates to identify equipment needing attention.
Changes Over Time
Review recorded activity alongside service history. A changed field, a service event and a physical handoff answer different questions, so establish which records your team needs to maintain.

Ownership and Service Relationships

Your Inventory.
Your Service Choices.

Standalone Asset is your organization’s item, material and business account. A service provider’s customer portal represents your relationship with that provider.

You choose which equipment to request service for. That relationship does not automatically share your whole inventory. Confirm the supported exchange, records and permissions with the chosen provider during your pilot.

Employee Issue and Return · Confirm in Pilot

Make Each Handoff Clear.

For teams issuing equipment to employees, the useful record identifies the recipient, the issue and the return. Discuss the required evidence, including any barcode identification or before-and-after photos, as part of your Asset pilot.

Confirm that the handoff records and permissions meet your needs before relying on this workflow. A recorded location or status change alone does not establish an employee issue or return.
