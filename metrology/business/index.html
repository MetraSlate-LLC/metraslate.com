The economics of the laboratory

Know What the Business Earns. Know What It Costs.

Accounting belongs beside the work that creates the revenue and consumes the resources.

An invoice total is only part of the story. Standard brings revenue, cost of service, operating expenses and net income into the same platform as calibration, purchasing and customer work.

Accounts grounded in transactions
Invoices, customer payments, purchase receipts and vendor bills feed the journal. Financial statements read the posted entries, keeping the business view tied to the transactions behind it.
Cost of service and overhead
The income statement separates cost of service from operating expenses, with month, fiscal-year-to-date and available prior-year comparisons. Read the cost of delivering the work alongside the cost of running the company.
Working time, not waiting time
Calibration start, pause, resume and completion record working-time spans. A run left over a weekend does not need to be treated as continuous bench labor; unfinished spans remain identifiable for review.
Profit and cash are different questions
Use the income statement, balance sheet and cash-flow statement together. Follow receivables and payables as well as earnings, so completed work and money received do not become the same number.
