Calculation and process reference

How the workflow operates.

Standard places a calculation and process reference beside its measurement tools. Each entry identifies what the calculation is for, the quantity it reports, its required inputs and conditions, and the mathematical operation it performs. A worked numerical chain lets the reader follow the inputs through intermediate values to the reported output.

The reference library covers supported calculations and planned methods. Each reference is explanatory guidance, not evidence that every described method is implemented or available in the pilot. Match it to the actual tool and retained result shown in your evaluation.

A laboratory can review the method before configuring a test, then open the same reference while inspecting a result or investigating a changed number. Formula, units and validity remain visible together, reducing the risk that a familiar statistic is applied to a different reported quantity or beyond the conditions the method supports.

In everyday use

What your team can do.

Follow the record, the decision and the person responsible for the next step.

Purpose identifies the question the tool answers.
Read the intended use and the reported quantity, including the distinction between a mean, a sample standard deviation, mathematical RSS and measurement uncertainty.
Inputs retain their meaning and origin.
Inspect the required quantities, units, raw observations, source documents and evidence before accepting the calculation.
Assumptions define the valid application.
Review independence or covariance, statistical conditions, required sample structure and the operating range for which the selected method applies.
Formulas expose the calculation chain.
Follow the declared equation, sensitivity, combination, coverage and reporting operations with their intermediate values.
Numerical cases make the method reviewable.
Compare a worked input-to-output chain with the selected calculation version and its declared precision and rounding boundaries.
Authoritative references retain their edition.
Open the relevant NIST, JCGM or calibration-policy reference and identify the source version used by the method entry.
Guidance and result evidence keep separate roles.
The reference explains the calculation. The result retains the model revision, inputs and evidence that were applied to the work.
Limits prevent silent changes of meaning.
Check the method's conditions and named refusals before using a result as an uncertainty, capability claim, study conclusion or conformity decision.

Method references

Read the guidance behind the calculation.

Review the source publication and edition alongside the model, inputs and evidence retained with your result.

  • NIST TN 1297, Section 3: Type A Evaluation of Standard Uncertainty

    Statistical evaluation from observations and the reported quantity.

    1994 edition, web section accessed 2026-10-21
  • NIST TN 1297, Appendix A: Law of Propagation of Uncertainty

    Measurement model, sensitivity, covariance and the standard uncertainty of a mean under the stated independent-observation conditions.

    1994 edition, equations A-1 to A-5, web section accessed 2026-10-21
  • NIST TN 1297, Appendix B: Coverage Factors

    Coverage-factor procedure and its assumptions, rather than a universal equivalence between a fixed k and a confidence level.

    1994 edition, web section accessed 2026-10-21
  • NIST/SEMATECH Engineering Statistics Handbook, Section 2.4: Gauge R&R Studies

    Study design, data collection, repeatability, reproducibility, stability and bias investigations.

    Chapter 2 publication 2003, online section accessed 2026-10-21
  • ILAC P14:09/2020: Measurement Uncertainty in Calibration

    Authoritative policy source for calibration uncertainty and CMC context.

    P14:09/2020, official policy register accessed 2026-10-21
  • JCGM 106:2012: The Role of Measurement Uncertainty in Conformity Assessment

    Decision-rule and conformity-assessment reference alongside the declared measurement model.

    JCGM 106:2012, official publication page accessed 2026-10-21
