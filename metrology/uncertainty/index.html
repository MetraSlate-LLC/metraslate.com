Standard performs the uncertainty evaluation for its calibration record. It resolves the contributions appropriate to the measurement, adds the configured DUT-resolution contribution and calculates the result under the selected service policy. The technician and reviewer can inspect the calculation at the point where it is used.

The result retains more than a final uncertainty number. Contributions, units, sensitivity, specification identity, coverage, TUR, guardband, decision outcome and reporting values provide the evidence needed to understand how the laboratory reached its conclusion.

A reviewer can trace each contribution.

A reference standard's contribution begins with its attributed identity and the specification applicable to the point. The retained trace shows the authored curve, the operating value and unit, coverage or distribution treatment, sensitivity and the contribution carried into the DUT quantity.

When the standard measures a different quantity, the declared measurement relationship supplies the transfer. A missing relationship or missing specification produces a named refusal. The evaluator does not present an incomplete contribution set as a complete uncertainty budget.

The arithmetic can be inspected at the measurement point.

The point inspector separates setup from calculation. Its calculation view shows reference-standard contributions, process uncertainty, the DUT-resolution contribution, combined standard uncertainty, coverage and reported expanded uncertainty. The same view places limits, guardband, TUR and the decision basis alongside the calculation.

For example, independent standard uncertainties of 1.20, 0.80 and 0.60 microvolts combine to approximately 1.56 microvolts. With fixed k = 2, the expanded uncertainty is approximately 3.12 microvolts before the selected reporting convention. The laboratory approves the contributors, sensitivity assumptions and coverage appropriate to its method.

The measurement and its decision retain their policy.

Service policy controls how the supported uncertainty model reports its result and how the decision uses it. Standard retains the coverage factor, coverage probability, TUR basis, guardband, acceptance interval and the resulting outcome rather than collapsing them into a single pass flag.

Where CMC-floor enforcement is enabled, Standard checks the reported expanded uncertainty against an applicable scope claim. Missing scope coverage and a reported value tighter than the matched claim remain meaningful conditions for the laboratory to review.

Completed calculations preserve the basis used at the time.

Completion retains the raw and evaluated readings, contribution rows, calculation values, policy results and selected specification snapshots with the event. Later reference-data changes therefore do not silently replace the evidence behind the completed result. Historical records can also identify calculation detail that was not recorded at the time.

When an external execution system is connected, its measurements enter as attributed observations. Standard performs the uncertainty evaluation for the governing record. Any supplied external calculation evidence keeps its origin so the reviewer can distinguish the supporting material from Standard's reported result.

Repeated observations retain the readings and the quantity being reported.

A test point can specify the required reading count and retain every raw observation against its point, side, unit and capture context. As-found and as-left series remain distinct. The reviewer can inspect the observations, count, mean, sample standard deviation and the treatment of excluded readings instead of receiving only an aggregated number.

The author explicitly identifies whether the tested result is the mean, the sample standard deviation itself or another defined output. For n independent observations under the same conditions, the repeatability contribution to the mean is s divided by √n, where s is the sample standard deviation. Reporting s as the tested result uses a model for the uncertainty of that estimate; it does not reuse the uncertainty of the mean.

Standard accounts for repeatability, shared reference contributions and declared correlation in the selected model. A calibration contribution shared by all observations does not become independent each time the technician repeats a reading. The calculation therefore does not simply average individual uncertainties or divide every contribution by √n.

For readings of 10.001, 10.002, 10.000, 10.003, 10.004 and 10.002 V, the mean is 10.002 V and the sample standard deviation is approximately 0.001414 V. Under the independent-repeatability model, the Type A contribution to that mean is approximately 0.00057735 V. Shared contributions remain in the model at their appropriate sensitivity and correlation.

Sensitivity and covariance state how the inputs affect the result.

The measurement model names the output quantity and the input quantities used to determine it. Each contribution retains its unit, standard uncertainty and sensitivity to the output. Where two inputs share a source or effect, the author declares their correlation or covariance and its supporting basis.

For first-order propagation, Standard evaluates uc²(y) = Σ ci² ui² + 2 Σi<j ci cj cov(xi,xj). Here ci is the sensitivity of y to input xi, ui is the standard uncertainty of xi, and cov(xi,xj) is the declared covariance. Independent inputs have zero covariance terms. The model retains its assumptions and validity limits so a covariance term, a missing relationship and an assumed independent input remain distinguishable.

The result exposes the contribution terms and cross-terms used in the combination. Reviewers can see whether a common standard, environmental source or correction influenced several observations, and can assess the selected model before accepting its expanded uncertainty and decision.

The evidence chain connects raw observations to the reported decision.

Inspection follows the raw observations through the selected specification, instance corrections, influence quantities, converted contributions, sensitivity and covariance, combined uncertainty, effective degrees of freedom, coverage and reported value. The applicable limits, CMC check and decision rule remain connected to that same evaluated result.

Each step identifies the definition and revision, source evidence, formula, units and intermediate values used. Reviewers can inspect the calculation and process reference from the point rather than reconstructing the method from a detached final number. Draft changes recompute their dependent results; released evidence retains the calculation basis used at release.

The laboratory can compare numerical reference cases with the implemented calculation and reporting boundaries. The reference identifies its purpose, inputs, assumptions, worked calculation and limits, while the retained result identifies the specific model and evidence used for the customer’s work.

In everyday use

What your team can do.

Follow the record, the decision and the person responsible for the next step.

The attributed standards determine the contribution basis.
Standard resolves the relevant specification and retains the curve, units, operating coordinate, distribution or divisor, sensitivity and resulting contribution.
Resolution is part of the point budget.
The configured DUT resolution adds its contribution through a rectangular, triangular or explicitly configured divisor treatment.
The declared model governs combination and coverage.
Combine independent contributions through their sensitivities and include declared covariance where inputs share an effect. The chosen method determines coverage, effective degrees of freedom and reporting precision.
The reported value retains its calculation basis.
Full calculated expanded uncertainty and the reported uncertainty remain distinct, so reporting precision does not replace the underlying result.
The decision uses the laboratory's configured policy.
Coverage, TUR basis, guardband, acceptance limits, gates and the optional CMC-floor check form part of the evaluation context.
Completion preserves the evidence used.
The finished record keeps observations, component values, specification snapshots and decision evidence together for review and certificate lineage.
Repeated readings preserve the declared output.
Configure the reading count, retain each observation by point and side, and identify whether the reported quantity is a mean, sample standard deviation or another authored result.
Shared effects remain part of the model.
Retain sensitivity, correlation and covariance so repeated use of the same reference or correction does not falsely reduce a shared contribution.

    Statistical evaluation from observations and the reported quantity.

    1994 edition, web section accessed 2026-10-21
  • NIST TN 1297, Appendix A: Law of Propagation of Uncertainty

    Measurement model, sensitivity, covariance and the standard uncertainty of a mean under the stated independent-observation conditions.

    1994 edition, equations A-1 to A-5, web section accessed 2026-10-21
  • NIST TN 1297, Appendix B: Coverage Factors

    Coverage-factor procedure and its assumptions, rather than a universal equivalence between a fixed k and a confidence level.

    1994 edition, web section accessed 2026-10-21

    Authoritative policy source for calibration uncertainty and CMC context.

    P14:09/2020, official policy register accessed 2026-10-21
  • JCGM 106:2012: The Role of Measurement Uncertainty in Conformity Assessment

    Decision-rule and conformity-assessment reference alongside the declared measurement model.

    JCGM 106:2012, official publication page accessed 2026-10-21
