CMC budgets, observations, uncertainty and the issued record answer different questions. Explore the supporting evidence at each stage.

Build a contributor budget, compare the supported capability with the claim, and retain the calculation behind the technical definition.

Explore CMC and Scope Preparation
Contributor basis
Record contributor values, distributions, sensitivity and degrees of freedom for the low and high endpoints of a budget.
Claimed and calculated capability
Inspect the calculated endpoints and the claimed-versus-proven comparison before publishing the exact technical basis as a reusable curve. Technical publication does not grant accreditation.
Scope lifecycle
Scope submission, review and publication controls are in development and validation. Discuss the requirements for your laboratory’s scope and accreditation evidence during the pilot.

Review, Issue and Retain the Record.

Completion, human review, document rendering and customer release are distinct steps. Preserve the measurement basis and the history of the certificate that follows.

Explore Calibration Records
Complete and review
Retain as-found and as-left readings, limits, standards and the calculation basis for review. Confirm reviewer and signatory permissions for the laboratory’s configuration.
Render and release
Produce the certificate from its retained record and make the released document available to authorized customers. A completed measurement alone is not a released certificate.
Amend or withdraw
Keep amendment and withdrawal history tied to the issued record. Configured review exceptions need their own authorization and audit trail.
