01 · Customer Request to Assigned Work

Know What Arrived and What Happens Next.

A pressure gauge arrives for calibration. Its estimate, customer approval and equipment identity establish what the laboratory needs to do.

Request and Estimate

Capture the customer’s requirements, prepare and revise an estimate, retain approval and convert accepted service lines into a work order.

Equipment Intake

Receive each instrument against the order. Keep its asset number, serial number, service history and current status together.

Assignments and Holds

Assign responsibility and follow each instrument’s progress. Keep a customer decision or other hold visible beside the work that can proceed.

Explore Requests and Work Orders

02 · Prepare, Measure and Evaluate

From an Approved Template to a Defensible Result.

Use approved templates and attributed reference standards. Prepare CMC budgets, record as-found and as-left measurements, and evaluate uncertainty and guardbanding at each point.

Define the Method Once. Control Its Revision.

Approved datasheets define measurement points, units, tolerances and resolution. A calibration run retains the serving revision used for the work.

Measurement WorkflowUncertainty and GuardbandingCMC Calculation Detail

03 · Review and Certificates

Review the Record. Release the Certificate.

The reviewer needs the evidence behind the result: observations, limits, standards, uncertainty and the method used. Certificate review and release have their own controlled steps.

Retain the Measurement Basis

Keep as-found and as-left readings, reference standards and the evaluation basis with the completed run.

Review and Authorize

Route the record through the configured review and signatory permissions before issuing the certificate.

Issue and Preserve History

Render the certificate, release it to the authorized customer and retain amendment or withdrawal history.

Explore Review and Certificates

04 · Customer Delivery and Business Follow-Through

Keep the Customer Informed. See the Business Behind the Work.

The service relationship continues through released certificates, equipment due dates, invoices and balances. Financial reporting brings revenue and recorded costs into view.

Give Customers a View of Their Service.

Authorized customers can follow their equipment and work, respond to decisions, retrieve released certificates and view invoices within their laboratory relationship.

Operating Cost Visibility

Read Revenue Alongside the Cost of Service.

Income statements group recorded revenue, cost of service and operating expenses. Review those totals and net income for the reporting period, with accounting records behind them.
