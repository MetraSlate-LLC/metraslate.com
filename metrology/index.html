The Measurement Engine.

In most systems the measurement is something the software stores. In Standard it is what the software is built around: the engine computes the uncertainty, applies the decision rule and produces the verdict inside the same transaction that records the reading, and everything else is arranged around it.

The decision rule

Guardbanded, Not Guessed.

Every reading carries its uncertainty into the verdict. When the test uncertainty ratio drops below 4:1, the guardband tightens the acceptance limit by a share of that uncertainty rather than setting it aside, and the math travels with the record.

The bench

The Math is the Product.

In this industry the number is the deliverable. Standard treats it that way.

When a technician records a reading, the engine computes the GUM uncertainty budget on the spot, Welch-Satterthwaite effective degrees of freedom and all, inside the same transaction that stores the value. The test uncertainty ratio and the Z540.3 guardband follow, and the verdict is made by the decision rule, with the math attached to the record forever. Values are exact decimals from the wire to the database. Nothing in the measurement path ever passes through floating point, because a rounding error in the seventh digit is still an error.

Automatic Uncertainty

GUM uncertainty is computed at the point of capture, inside the same transaction that records the reading. Not in a spreadsheet after the fact.

Guardbanded Decisions

Z540.3 guardbanding runs as the decision rule, not a habit. Every pass and fail carries the math that produced it, attached to the record.

Validated Against the Literature

The engines are checked against published vectors from the GUM, ILAC-G8, Z540.3 and the Dobbert guardbanding literature. When the standards and our output disagree, the build fails.

Service Levels Carry the Policy

Guardband method, coverage factor, resolution handling and pricing travel together as a service level, so the lab's policy is applied by the system, not remembered by the technician.

CMC and Scope, Same System

CMC budgets on the A2LA R205 methodology push into your specs, and your scope of accreditation lives beside the work it governs.

The composition

How the Edition is Put Together.

The industry knowledge is the honey that bonds the industry to the surface.

The platform underneath is service agnostic: assets, work orders, workflows, portals, documents, reports. The Metrology Edition is what happens when the industry's own structure is bound into those pieces instead of layered over them. An asset stops being a row in an equipment list and becomes a calibratable instrument, with a specification behind it, an interval in front of it, and every event it has been through attached to it. The specification carries the formulas, so a manufacturer's accuracy statement becomes a computable tolerance at each test point rather than a line in a PDF.

The service level carries the policy that decides the verdict, and the work order routes the instrument through the flows your quality system requires. The compliance structure sits across all of it: qualifications gate who may sign, the audit trail runs under every field, and the accreditation scope governs what the record is allowed to claim. Nothing is copied between the pieces, because there is only one record.

Where this is going

Traceability as Data, Not Paperwork.

NIST traceability today lives in PDF text and test numbers, and every audit is a paper chase to rebuild a chain that was never stored as data. Standard treats traceability as data instead: the sealed certificate is already a machine-verifiable link, not a document to hunt down. The full chain, field instrument to working standard to reference standard to the test number, extends that same shape, with distributed-ledger anchoring under evaluation for the step where separate organizations verify each other's links.
