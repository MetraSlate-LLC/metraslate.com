The customer journey

From First Conversation to Accounts Receivable.

A new lead and a returning customer can enter through different doors. The work still belongs together.

  1. 01

    A Lead or a Customer Request

    Start with a lead and opportunity in Sales, or an estimate request from an existing customer through the portal. Bring the instruments, requested service and customer details into the conversation.

  2. 02

    Estimate and Approval

    Price the work against the instruments it covers. Keep the customer’s approval, purchase order number and supporting note with the estimate before it becomes a work order.

  3. 03

    Receive and Assign

    Record custody when the instrument arrives. Give each work-order line its service level, assignee and promise date, and follow its progress through the laboratory.

  4. 04

    Calibrate and Review

    Work from a structured datasheet with test points, readings, standards and measurement policy. Keep the technical record together as the work moves into review and certificate release.

  5. 05

    Resolve the Exceptions

    A failed unit may need a customer decision, a repair estimate, parts or an outside vendor. Record the answer and the next step against the work instead of losing the handoff in a separate list.

  6. 06

    Deliver and Invoice

    Plan the return, record the shipment and follow the invoice and accounts receivable. The customer’s equipment, the work performed and the commercial record remain connected.

Root cause and corrective action

Follow the Finding Until the Fix is Proven.

A closed ticket is not the same thing as a resolved problem.

Root-cause analysis
Build a cause tree from the finding, follow each why and identify the root cause. Keep the analysis with its evidence, affected instruments and customer-notification decisions.
Corrective action
Assign the fix, its due date and a separate effectiveness check. Record whether the action actually worked, and keep that answer with the original finding.
Closure with conditions
Standard checks unresolved actions, affected-item decisions and owed customer notices before closing a finding. Laboratory policy can also require a named root cause and an effectiveness answer; those rules apply to automated status changes too.

Evidence for review

Keep the Record as It Was Completed.

Answer which readings, calculation inputs and decisions supported that result.

Frozen calibration evidence
Completion records the point results, uncertainty components, attributed standards, exclusions and supporting snapshots together. Historical results retain their recorded calculation basis when live reference data changes.
Corrections with a history
Completed calibration events and their result records are append-only at the application database boundary. A correction creates a successor linked to the earlier event, preserving the original rather than silently replacing it.
Review and release evidence
Technician signing, review approval and sealing are separate recorded actions. The laboratory can follow the calibration evidence and the decisions behind certificate release when preparing for an audit.

When the work changes

A Real Lab Has Exceptions.

Repairs, outside vendors and customer decisions are part of the job.

Customer decisions
Open a decision from the work-order line and record what failed and what the customer wants to do. A repair answer can lead to a repair estimate, with its own pricing and approval.
Vendor custody
Use Send To Vendor and Back From Vendor to record an instrument’s outside leg. Keep that movement distinct from the final shipment back to the customer.
Held work
See work awaiting a customer answer, parts, backorder resolution or outsourcing. Follow the individual line to the context needed to move it forward.
Quality follow-through
Keep nonconformities, corrective actions, affected items and dispositions connected to the records they concern, with owners and recorded decisions.

The test definition
The instrument’s datasheet brings test points, tolerances, resolution and formulas to the run. Asset templates describe the model family and its specification.
The measurement policy
Service levels keep the uncertainty, coverage, decision rule, guardband and rounding choices together. The laboratory can review the policy the work uses.

The review
Read the measurement and its basis together before certificate release. Missing information and unresolved conditions need an answer, not an invented result.

Smart documents

Your Certificate. Your Template. Your Calibration Data.

Document Creator is the template authoring workspace for the documents your laboratory issues.

Design the certificate once, then populate it from the calibration record. Place your logo, customer and instrument details, measurement results and supporting text on the page. The template controls the presentation; the underlying record supplies the values.

Data-bound fields
Bind document fields to the document type’s supported schema instead of retyping values. Typed bindings distinguish text, dates and other supported field types; validation checks whether the template uses them correctly.
Repeating results tables
Define the columns and formatting once. A repeating table renders the available result rows, so a certificate can show the test points in its calibration record without a separately drawn row for every point.
Page layout and preview
Edit on the page canvas with content and properties together. Control page size, margins, headers, footers and table layout, and inspect a populated preview before publication.
Versioned templates
Keep an editable draft, validate it and publish a template version. Template activation determines which published design is used; publishing a template is separate from reviewing and releasing a customer’s certificate.

The economics of the laboratory

Know What the Business Earns. Know What It Costs.

Accounting belongs beside the work that creates the revenue and consumes the resources.

An invoice total is only part of the story. Standard brings revenue, cost of service, operating expenses and net income into the same platform as calibration, purchasing and customer work.

Accounts grounded in transactions
Invoices, customer payments, purchase receipts and vendor bills feed the journal. Financial statements read the posted entries, keeping the business view tied to the transactions behind it.
Cost of service and overhead
The income statement separates cost of service from operating expenses, with month, fiscal-year-to-date and available prior-year comparisons. Read the cost of delivering the work alongside the cost of running the company.
Working time, not waiting time
Calibration start, pause, resume and completion record working-time spans. A run left over a weekend does not need to be treated as continuous bench labor; unfinished spans remain identifiable for review.
Profit and cash are different questions
Use the income statement, balance sheet and cash-flow statement together. Follow receivables and payables as well as earnings, so completed work and money received do not become the same number.
