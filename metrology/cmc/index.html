Pilot / Early Access Standard by MetraSlate: Metrology Edition. How Evaluation Works

Standard brings CMC budget calculation, scope entries and measurement uncertainty into the calibration workflow. A technical manager can maintain the evidence behind a claimed range while technicians and reviewers inspect the basis used for individual measurements.

The work begins with a defined reference standard and measurement function. The laboratory records the source or measure role, range, unit, optional frequency band, and claimed gain, offset and coverage factor. This gives the capability a numerical definition that can be checked against its supporting budget.

Contributor Budget

A CMC budget retains contributor values at a low and a high point. Sensitivity and the selected distribution convert those inputs into standard uncertainty contributions. Supported inputs include normal-expanded, rectangular, triangular, U-shaped and standard-deviation values. An expanded-uncertainty input retains the coverage factor at which it was stated.

Standard combines the uncorrelated contributions by root-sum-square and calculates effective degrees of freedom. The scope-budget calculation can use a fixed coverage factor or its Student-t coverage basis. It applies one coverage factor across the two endpoints and rounds the expanded endpoint uncertainties upward to the supported publication precision before fitting the curve.

Claim and Publication

A capability claim can become unsupported when a contributor changes. Standard distinguishes a claim that has no computed budget from one supported by the calculation and one that undercuts its supporting uncertainty. A refusal identifies the numerical disagreement so the laboratory can review the claim or its contributors.

Publishing places the fitted curve into the reference standard’s specification catalog. The curve retains its coverage and budget relationship. This is a technical publication; it is not an accrediting body’s approval of the laboratory’s scope.

CMC Calculator

The engineering-calculator library also includes a CMC Uncertainty Budget tool. Its survey, low-point budget, high-point budget and equation views let the user examine contributor values, sensitivity, distributions, degrees of freedom, endpoint uncertainty and the fitted gain and offset.

Calculator drafts and saved results are stored locally in the user's browser. A calculator study is useful for examining the numbers; the scope-entry budget and controlled publication keep the laboratory's reusable capability definition attached to its reference standard.

Measurement Review

A published reference-standard curve supplies a contribution to later point evaluation. The calibration record then combines the actual attributed standards with the DUT-resolution contribution and the selected measurement policy. This connects capability preparation to the uncertainty reported for the work.

The laboratory can revisit contributors and ranges as its standards and methods change, while reviewing completed results against their retained calculation evidence. Budget authoring supports preparation and maintenance of scope evidence; it does not automatically submit an accrediting body's complete scope document.
