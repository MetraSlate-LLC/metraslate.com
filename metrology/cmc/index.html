Standard brings CMC budget calculation, scope entries and measurement uncertainty into the calibration workflow. A technical manager can maintain the evidence behind a claimed range while technicians and reviewers inspect the basis used for individual measurements.

The work begins with a defined reference standard and measurement function. The laboratory records the source or measure role, range, unit, optional frequency band, and claimed gain, offset and coverage factor. This gives the capability a numerical definition that can be checked against its supporting budget.

The contributor budget explains the capability.

A CMC budget retains contributor values at a low and a high point. Sensitivity and the selected distribution convert those inputs into standard uncertainty contributions. Supported inputs include normal-expanded, rectangular, triangular, U-shaped and standard-deviation values. An expanded-uncertainty input retains the coverage factor at which it was stated.

Standard combines the uncorrelated contributions by root-sum-square and calculates effective degrees of freedom. The scope-budget calculation can use a fixed coverage factor or its Student-t coverage basis. It applies one coverage factor across the two endpoints and rounds the expanded endpoint uncertainties upward to the supported publication precision before fitting the curve.

The claim and its supporting calculation remain connected.

A capability claim can become unsupported when a contributor changes. Standard distinguishes a claim that has no computed budget from one supported by the calculation and one that undercuts its supporting uncertainty. A refusal identifies the numerical disagreement so the laboratory can review the claim or its contributors.

Publishing places the fitted curve into the reference standard's specification catalog. The curve retains its coverage and budget relationship, and replacing it creates a controlled release. A run can use the specification release under which it was committed, while completed measurement records retain the basis actually used.

A built-in CMC calculator makes the arithmetic visible.

The engineering-calculator library also includes a CMC Uncertainty Budget tool. Its survey, low-point budget, high-point budget and equation views let the user examine contributor values, sensitivity, distributions, degrees of freedom, endpoint uncertainty and the fitted gain and offset.

Calculator drafts and saved results are stored locally in the user's browser. A calculator study is useful for examining the numbers; the scope-entry budget and controlled publication keep the laboratory's reusable capability definition attached to its reference standard.

Capability work continues into measurement review.

A published reference-standard curve supplies a contribution to later point evaluation. The calibration record then combines the actual attributed standards with the DUT-resolution contribution and the selected measurement policy. This connects capability preparation to the uncertainty reported for the work.

The laboratory can revisit contributors and ranges as its standards and methods change, while reviewing completed results against their retained calculation evidence. Budget authoring supports preparation and maintenance of scope evidence; it does not automatically submit an accrediting body's complete scope document.

Manage the scope record from definition through publication.

The scope record brings its definition, editing, contributor budget, supporting evidence and review into one working path. The technical manager can inspect the reference standard, function, source or measure role, range, units and frequency conditions, then work through the assumptions supporting the claimed capability.

Edits retain their relationship to the budget and the material being reviewed. Supporting documents and calculation evidence give the reviewer the basis for accepting or returning the change. Publication makes the approved capability available through its controlled release, so the technician uses an identifiable definition rather than a separately maintained copy.

Preserve the exact scope and calculation basis used by the result.

A completed result retains the applicable scope revision, claimed range, CMC relationship and evaluation basis alongside the observations and uncertainty calculation. The retained record identifies the method, specification release, coverage and decision policy used for that measurement.

A later change to a range, contributor, curve or evaluation policy does not reinterpret the earlier result. The reviewer can explain why a capability applied at the time of the work and compare a correction with its predecessor using the evidence each record actually used.

Control when a capability becomes effective and who may change it.

Scope management distinguishes a working draft, a reviewed laboratory definition and the approved accreditation information recorded for the capability. Numerical support from a budget and accreditation approval have separate meanings. The record retains the responsible people, supporting approval and the conditions under which the entry applies.

Effective dates, expiry and supersession govern the usable revision. An authorized person can prepare a replacement and route it for review while the current approved entry remains identifiable. Permissions separate drafting, technical review, approval and publication, and the historical record retains which revision held each responsibility.

Carry scope treatment to each result and the certificate that reports it.

Scope is resolved for the actual result, with its function, role, range, units, frequency conditions and applicable approved definition. Results outside the approved scope remain identifiable through the worksheet, evaluation, technical review and certificate output rather than acquiring accredited status from the surrounding job.

A certificate can contain both accredited and non-accredited results with their treatment clearly identified. The accreditation statement and mark follow the results and conditions they apply to. A limited calibration describes the selected procedure points performed; that limited procedure coverage remains a separate fact from whether an individual result falls within the approved accredited scope.

Coverage and reporting follow one consistent numerical contract.

CMC authoring, the calculation tools and measurement evaluation preserve the meaning of each contributor. An input stated as expanded uncertainty keeps its stated coverage factor; a standard uncertainty keeps its distribution, sensitivity and degrees of freedom. Unit conversion preserves the difference between a measured quantity and an uncertainty magnitude.

Standard retains the combined standard uncertainty, expanded uncertainty, coverage probability, coverage factor and effective degrees of freedom as separate values. Where the selected method uses Welch-Satterthwaite and Student-t coverage, finite degrees of freedom determine the appropriate coverage step. Working values remain available before publication or reporting precision is applied.

A published capability curve and the uncertainty printed with a result are checked for their own purposes on the declared coverage basis. Standard retains the endpoint calculation, fitted curve, applicable range, rounding rule and comparison evidence, so a technical manager can trace a claim through calculation and publication without losing the assumptions behind it.

Each contributor retains its assumptions.

The endpoints support a reusable curve.
Standard calculates the endpoint budgets and fits a gain-plus-offset uncertainty curve for the declared band.
The claim can be checked against its support.
Claim states distinguish an uncomputed budget, a claim supported by its budget and a claim that is tighter than the configured evidence supports.
Publication creates a controlled specification release.

The laboratory keeps responsibility for accreditation.
