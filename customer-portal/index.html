Pilot / Early Access Standard by MetraSlate: Metrology Edition. How Evaluation Works

Equipment and Due Dates

The equipment record brings together the instrument's identity, site, due context, previous service and current work. Customers can identify what needs attention and begin the next service request from the instruments already on their account. The laboratory receives equipment context with the request.

Access follows the customer relationship and the contact's authorized sites. The laboratory can give a contact a useful view of their own equipment, work and documents while retaining control over information belonging to another site or account.

Requests and Quotes

Customers select equipment, describe the required service and add the information the laboratory needs to price it. They can save and revise an unsent request, then send it when the instrument list and service needs are ready.

When the laboratory issues its quotation, the authorized contact reviews the offered service and the issued document. Approval and PO information remain with the quotation and account. A revised quotation retains its own commercial context, so the service agreement has a clear basis.

Pickup Requests

Customers request collection at an authorized site and provide a preferred day, collection window and instructions. The request becomes the pickup record the laboratory manages, with the site's address and customer context already connected.

Customers can follow the pickup information shared with them as the laboratory confirms and plans the collection. The requested date remains distinguishable from the laboratory's confirmed operational arrangement.

Progress and Decisions

The portal shows the service status shared for an instrument and the work order it belongs to. Customers can inspect the related visit, service progress and equipment history, then discuss that specific work with the laboratory.

When failed equipment needs a decision, the laboratory presents its permitted next steps. The authorized contact responds against the affected decision, and the answer stays with the service record. A priced repair or additional service follows its required commercial approval before the additional amount becomes collectible.

Released Certificates

After the laboratory releases a certificate, the customer can open it from the related equipment and service context. The equipment history provides a route back to earlier released evidence when a customer prepares for an audit, reviews a result or plans the next calibration.

Issued quotations and invoices retain their document context as well. The customer can retrieve the relevant paper while ownership, site access and release controls govern what is available.

Conversations and Account

Customers can converse with the laboratory about equipment, service and orders, and provide feedback about their experience. The conversation reaches the laboratory's customer-service workflow, where staff can take responsibility and involve the colleague needed for the next answer.

The account also provides issued invoices, balances and purchase-order context. Where the laboratory offers customer store listings, customers can browse the permitted goods, maintain a cart, choose supported shipping or collection and follow the resulting order.
